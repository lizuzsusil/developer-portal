# What is a mini app?

*No technical knowledge needed. If you are here to write code, start at the
[Platform Overview](/docs/overview) instead.*

Sewa is the Government of Sri Lanka's citizen app. A **mini app** is one
service inside it — renewing a licence, checking a fuel quota, paying a fee.

Citizens open Sewa, tap your service, and use it. They install nothing extra
and sign in only once, to Sewa itself.

## Why do it this way

| Reason | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Citizens already have it** | No new app to market, download or explain. |
| **They are already signed in** | Sewa has verified who they are. You never build a login. |
| **One place for government** | Someone renewing a licence can also pay a fee, without leaving. |
| **You keep control** | Your service, your data, your release schedule. |

## Who does what

**Your agency provides**

- The screens the citizen sees.
- The service behind them, and the data it uses.
- Someone to keep it running.

**Sewa provides**

- The app citizens already have, and the citizen's verified identity.
- Hosting and delivery of your screens.
- Device features such as camera and location, where you are permitted them.
- Notifications when something finishes.

## What a citizen experiences

1. Opens Sewa and taps your service on the home screen.
2. Your screens appear inside Sewa, already knowing who they are.
3. They do the thing — check a quota, submit a form, pay.
4. If it takes time, they get a notification and can come back later.

They are never sent to a website and never asked to sign in again.

## What it takes to add a service

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Build** | A small web app and the service behind it. Weeks, not months, for something focused. |
| **Review** | Sewa checks the code and agrees what citizen data you may use. |
| **Launch** | Sewa publishes it. It can be switched off instantly if something is wrong. |

## Questions worth settling early

- **What is the smallest useful version?** One task done well beats five done
  partly. Mini apps are meant to be narrow.
- **How certain must you be who the citizen is?** Checking a quota needs less
  certainty than transferring money. That choice affects how many citizens can
  use it on day one.
- **What happens when it is slow or down?** Some services answer in seconds,
  others take minutes. Both are supported; they feel different to the citizen
  and need deciding up front.
- **Who answers when a citizen has a problem?** Sewa runs the app. Your agency
  runs the service inside it.
