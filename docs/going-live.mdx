# Going live

Testing in the playground registers your mini app against a local or shared
development stack. Production is a separate, reviewed process.

## What the platform needs from you

| Item | What you provide |
|---|---|
| **Agency** | The organisation that owns the mini app. Created once, by the Sewa team. |
| **Mini app frontend bundle** | An ES-module build exporting `mount`. You submit it; you do not host it. |
| **Mini app backend** | Source, or a deployment descriptor, so the platform can deploy and run it. You do not supply a URL. |
| **Capabilities** | The namespaces you need, and why. You are granted only these. |
| **Assurance class** | How strongly the citizen must be verified before your app opens. |

Two things you do **not** provide:

- **The mini app id.** The platform generates it when your mini app is created,
  and it identifies you on every call from then on.
- **The backend URL.** The platform deploys your backend and records its
  address itself, so it can move or rebuild it without involving you.

## Assurance classes

Sewa verifies citizens to different levels, and your mini app declares the
minimum it needs. A citizen below that level is asked to step up before your app
loads, so you never have to check it yourself.

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `T0` | Anonymous or self-asserted |
| `T1` | Phone number verified |
| `T2` | Identity verified |
| `T3` | Identity verified on a bound device |

Ask for the lowest class that your service genuinely requires. A higher class
turns citizens away at the door.

## Where your bundle lives

Agencies never self-host mini app bundles. You submit a build; the platform
scans it and publishes it to its own CDN, and the manifest that points at it is
signed. At mount time Sewa verifies that signature and then checks the
bundle's hash before running a line of your code.

Two consequences worth planning for:

- **Shipping an update means submitting a new bundle**, not deploying to your
  own host. Build that into your release process.
- **A mismatched hash is refused**, so the bundle you submit is the bundle that
  runs. Nothing can be patched in place afterwards.

The [playground](/docs/playground) is the one exception, for development only:
there you point Sewa at a URL you serve yourself.

## What your bundle must be

| Requirement | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Format** | A single ES module exporting `mount` |
| **Self-contained** | All code and assets in the bundle. Nothing fetched from another CDN at runtime |
| **No runtime injection** | No `eval`, no scripts added to the page after load. The scan rejects it and signing makes it pointless |
| **Versioned** | Every submission is a new version. Bundles are never edited in place |

Fonts, images and styles ship inside the bundle. A bundle that reaches out to a
third-party origin at runtime will be refused, because the signature only
covers what you submitted.

## Versions and rollout

| Control | How it works |
|---|---|
| **SDK version** | You declare the minimum SDK your bundle needs. A shell older than that will not load it |
| **Rollout** | A new version can go to a percentage of citizens first |
| **Kill switch** | Your mini app can be disabled immediately, without a release. It disappears from the home screen |

A disabled mini app stops being offered. Citizens already inside finish what
they are doing; nothing new starts. Plan for your backend to keep answering
briefly after you are switched off.

## The sequence

1. **Your agency is created** in the Sewa Back Office by the platform team.
2. **You submit the mini app** with the details above.
3. **Your bundle is scanned**, then published to the platform CDN.
4. **Your backend is deployed** by the platform from what you supplied, and
   its address is recorded against your mini app.
5. **Go live** is approved by the platform team, and the mini app appears for
   citizens. It can be switched off again at any time, without a release.

## Before you submit

- Your backend answers only Sewa, not the public internet.
- Your app works when a capability is refused.
- Your app works on a slow connection, and says something useful when a call fails.
- You have tested the whole flow in the [playground](/docs/playground).
- Your bundle exports `mount` and cleans up in `unmount`.
- Your backend reads its own configuration from the environment, because the
  platform decides where it runs.
